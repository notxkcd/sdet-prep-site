Questions with Answers

Beginner (Foundations) - Answers

1. What is Agile?

Answer: Agile is an iterative approach to software development and project management that helps teams deliver value to their customers faster and with fewer headaches. It focuses on continuous improvement and flexibility.

2. Explain Agile?

Answer: Agile is a mindset based on 12 principles and 4 values (defined in the Agile Manifesto). It prioritizes people, working software, and customer collaboration over rigid processes and documentation.

3. Explain Agile Scrum methodology?

Answer: Scrum is a specific framework within Agile. It uses fixed-length iterations called Sprints (usually 2-4 weeks) to deliver incremental chunks of a product. It involves specific roles (Scrum Master, Product Owner, Dev Team) and ceremonies.

4. What is Agile methodology?

Answer: It is a practice that promotes continuous iteration of development and testing throughout the software development lifecycle (SDLC) of the project.

5. Explain agile methodologies?

Answer: There are several frameworks under the Agile umbrella, including Scrum, Kanban, Lean, and Extreme Programming (XP). Scrum is the most popular for software development.

6. Explain agile methodologies in your project?

Answer: In our project, we follow Scrum. We have a product backlog, work in 2-week sprints, and have daily stand-ups to sync on progress and blockers.

7. What methodology do you use in your project?

Answer: We use the Agile Scrum methodology to ensure we can adapt to changing requirements and provide frequent releases to our stakeholders.

8. Why do we go for Agile?

Answer: We choose Agile because it allows for faster delivery, better quality (through continuous testing), and high flexibility to change requirements even late in development.

9. What is a story in Agile?

Answer: A “Story” (or User Story) is the smallest unit of work. It describes a feature from the perspective of the end-user (e.g., “As a user, I want to reset my password”).

10. What is a user story?

Answer: A user story is an informal, general explanation of a software feature written from the perspective of the end user or customer.

11. What is an epic?

Answer: An Epic is a large body of work that can be broken down into a number of smaller stories. It represents a significant feature or a module.

12. Explain epic and feature?

Answer: A Feature is a distinct functionality of the system. An Epic is a collection of related features or a very large feature that spans multiple sprints.

13. What is a sprint?

Answer: A Sprint is a short, time-boxed period when a scrum team works to complete a set amount of work.

14. What is the sprint size?

Answer: This refers to the number of story points or tasks a team commits to in a single sprint, based on their capacity.

15. What is the sprint duration?

Answer: In most industry standards, a sprint lasts between 2 to 4 weeks. In my project, we use a 2-week sprint cycle.

Intermediate (Ceremonies & Roles) - Answers

1. What are the agile ceremonies?

Answer: There are four main ceremonies:

  1. Sprint Planning
  2. Daily Stand-up
  3. Sprint Review
  4. Sprint Retrospective

2. Explain the agile ceremonies?

Answer:

  • Planning: Team decides what to work on.
  • Stand-up: Quick 15-min daily sync.
  • Review: Demo of completed work to stakeholders.
  • Retrospective: Team looks back to improve the next sprint.

3. What are the Scrum meetings?

Answer: These are the same as Agile ceremonies (Planning, Stand-up, Review, Retrospective) plus the optional but recommended Backlog Refinement.

4. What are the meetings you will attend in agile?

Answer: As a QA, I attend all four main ceremonies and the backlog grooming sessions.

5. Who will be conducting the sprint plan meeting and what you will do in the meeting?

Answer: Conducted by the Scrum Master. I (the QA) review the requirements, provide effort estimations, and identify testing risks for the stories.

6. What is discussed in the Scrum meeting?

Answer: In the Daily Scrum, each member answers:

  1. What did I do yesterday?
  2. What will I do today?
  3. Are there any blockers?

7. What is a sprint retrospective?

Answer: A meeting where the team discusses “What went well,” “What didn’t go well,” and “How to improve” after a sprint ends.

8. What is meant by a sprint retrospective meeting?

Answer: It is a meeting held at the end of every sprint to ensure the team is continuously improving its process.

9. What is the purpose of the retrospective meeting?

Answer: The primary purpose is Continuous Improvement—identifying process bottlenecks and actionable steps to fix them.

10. Who will participate in the retrospective meeting?

Answer: The entire Scrum Team: The Scrum Master, the Product Owner, and the Development/Testing Team.

11. When will the retrospective meeting happen?

Answer: It happens at the very end of the sprint, usually after the Sprint Review and before the next Sprint Planning.

12. What can the Scrum master do in that retrospective meeting?

Answer: The Scrum Master facilitates the meeting, ensures everyone is heard, and helps the team identify concrete improvement actions.

13. Explain about the Scrum master?

Answer: The Scrum Master is a Servant Leader who helps the team follow Scrum values and removes any “impediments” (blockers) that hinder progress.

14. What is refinement/grooming?

Answer: It is the process of keeping the backlog “healthy” by reviewing, detailing, and estimating upcoming stories.

15. What is sprint grooming?

Answer: Another term for Backlog Refinement, where the team ensures that stories for the upcoming sprints are “Ready” for development.

16. When do refinement and grooming happen in a sprint?

Answer: Usually in the middle of the current sprint to prepare for the next one.

17. Who will assign stories?

Answer: In true Scrum, stories aren’t assigned; the Team pulls them based on priority and capacity. However, the Product Owner sets the priority.

18. What will you do after being assigned the stories?

Answer: I analyze the requirements, write test scenarios, create test cases, and prepare test data for the upcoming development.

Advanced (Metrics & Practical Challenges) - Answers

1. How do you give story points?

Answer: We use Planning Poker. Points (usually Fibonacci: 1, 2, 3, 5, 8…) are assigned based on the Complexity, Effort, and Risk of the task.

2. How do you derive story points and how is it calculated?

Answer: It is not a direct time calculation. We compare a story against a “baseline” story. If the baseline is a 2, and the new one is twice as hard, it’s a 5.

3. How many hours are calculated for each story point?

Answer: Story points are about complexity, not hours. However, teams often find an average (e.g., 1 point might feel like 4-6 hours of work), but this varies per team.

4. Can you explain how you will calculate the story points?

Answer: By looking at:

  1. The amount of work.
  2. The technical complexity.
  3. The uncertainty or risks involved.

5. How will you confirm the complexity of a story?

Answer: By discussing it with the developers and the Product Owner during grooming to understand technical dependencies and data requirements.

6. What is velocity in Agile?

Answer: Velocity is the average number of story points a team completes in a sprint. It helps in planning future sprints.

7. What does the term “Velocity” refer to in Agile Testing?

Answer: It refers to the speed at which the team can deliver fully tested, “Done” software in a single iteration.

8. What is a burn down chart?

Answer: A graph showing how much work is remaining vs. time. It helps track if the team will finish the sprint scope on time.

9. What is productivity in Agile?

Answer: It’s measured by the team’s ability to deliver high-quality features that meet the Definition of Done (DoD) and satisfy the Product Owner.

10. What is PA planning?

Answer: Likely refers to Program Increment (PI) Planning in SAFe (Scaled Agile Framework), where multiple teams align on a common mission for the next 8-12 weeks.

11. What is the role of API testing in Agile/DevOps?

Answer: It provides fast feedback and allows testing to start earlier (Shift-Left), as APIs are often ready before the UI.

12. What are the artifacts of Agile?

Answer:

  1. Product Backlog
  2. Sprint Backlog
  3. Product Increment

13. What is your contribution in the RTM?

Answer: I ensure every requirement (story) is mapped to at least one test case, ensuring 100% test coverage.

14. Explain the purpose of RTM in Agile?

Answer: Even in Agile, RTM (Requirements Traceability Matrix) ensures that we haven’t missed testing any of the accepted user stories.

15. You have started working in an Agile method, and in between, you need some requirements. What will you do?

Answer: I would immediately bring it up in the Daily Stand-up or speak with the Product Owner to get clarification before proceeding.

16. Explain challenges you have faced in your experience and which agile meeting you shared them in and how it improved?

Answer: Example: “We lacked clear acceptance criteria.” I shared this in the Sprint Retrospective. As a result, we made it a rule that no story enters a sprint without a clear “Definition of Ready.”
