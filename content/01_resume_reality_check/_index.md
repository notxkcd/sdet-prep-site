## Recruiter's 30-Second Summary
"Mohammed is a Java-based QA Engineer with roughly 2.5 years of experience at a single company (Learning Links Foundation). He started in late 2022, which leaves a visible 2-year gap after his 2020 graduation. His tech stack is standard: Java, Selenium, Cucumber, and TestNG. He claims exposure to CI/CD (Jenkins) and API testing (Rest Assured), but his experience is limited to one specific EdTech project ('IntelliLearn'). He appears to be a functional tester who transitioned into automation."

## Your ACTUAL Level
**Junior SDET / Strong Manual Tester with Automation Skills**

You are not yet a "Mid-Level SDET" in the eyes of top-tier product companies. 
*   **Why?** You have only worked on one project. True mid-level SDETs have faced different challenges across varied domains or architectures. 
*   **The Good News:** You are perfect for roles looking for a "Junior Automation Engineer" or a "QA Engineer" who can handle both manual and automation tasks.

## Red Flags & Weak Areas
| 1.  **The 2020-2022 Gap:** You graduated in 2020 but started your first job in Dec 2022. Recruiters *will* ask about this. If you don't have a solid story (e.g., freelance, upskilling, personal reasons), it looks bad.
| 2.  **"Leading to promotion":** You mention learning Selenium led to a promotion. This implies a significant chunk of your 2.5 years was likely manual testing, and your *actual* automation experience might be less than 1.5 years.
| 3.  **Buzzword Stuffing:** Terms like "Data Warehousing" and "Decision support systems" seem out of place for a generic QA resume unless you heavily tested ETL pipelines (which isn't clearly described).
| 4.  **Generic "Proficiency":** Phrases like "Proficient in developing robust... scripts" are filler. Everyone says this. The proof is missing (e.g., "Reduced regression time by 40%").
