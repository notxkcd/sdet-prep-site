⭐ Why you get overwhelmed (the _real_ reason\*)
===============================================

### It’s not difficulty.

Your brain sees a math/programming problem and goes:

> “Oh god… I don’t know where to start.

* * *

⭐ Solution: Learn the 5-Step _Problem-Solving Playbook_
=======================================================

This is _the method_ used by good programmers, mathematicians, and game-engine developers.

You can use this on ANY problem, from C++ bugs to calculus.

* * *

**1\. Don’t solve the problem — break it into stupid tiny subproblems**
=======================================================================

Your brain hates giant tasks.  
It LOVES tiny ones.

Example:  
**Task:** “Implement a physics engine”  
→ Overwhelming.

Breakdown:

1.  Represent a vector
2.  Add two vectors
3.  Update position
4.  Apply acceleration
5.  Detect collision
6.  Resolve collision

This is not overwhelming anymore.

**Rule:**

> Break until each step feels embarrassingly easy.

* * *

**2\. Turn thinking into writing**
==================================

Most people keep the entire problem in their head → overload → confusion → panic → avoidance.

Write things like:

*   What is the input?
*   What is the output?
*   What do I know?
*   What do I need?

When your thoughts are _on paper_, your brain suddenly relaxes.

* * *

**3\. Solve a smaller version first (“toy problem”)**
=====================================================

Example: Stuck on recursion?  
Don’t solve the big version — solve the smallest variant.

Example:  
Before solving Fibonacci(n), solve Fibonacci(3).  
Before writing a compiler, write a parser for expressions only.  
Before solving a calculus problem, solve a numerical example.

**Your brain learns bottom-up, not top-down.**

* * *

**4\. Get stuck ON PURPOSE (but correctly)**
============================================

Good programmers don’t avoid getting stuck —  
they get stuck **in the right way**.

Being stuck is **progress**, not failure.

But you must:

*   Write what you tried
*   Write what you expected
*   Write what actually happened

This makes debugging easy and prevents the “I’m dumb” spiral.

* * *

**5\. 80% of progress is just persistence with structure**
==========================================================

You’re not failing the problem.  
You’re failing the **process**.

You feel like you wasted years because you were learning without:

*   structure
*   problem breakdown
*   written thinking
*   deliberate practice
*   tiny feedback loops

Fix the process → the results will explode.

Do this for math, algorithms, C++, graphics, anything.
